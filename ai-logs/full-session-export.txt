  ## 2. THE PROJECT: "Zen Stones" — Recursive Soundscapes                       
  =====================================================================         
  Concept: A calm riverside at dusk. River stones sit on 3 flat rock platforms. 
  The Tower of Hanoi                                                            
  recursion moves the stones one by one; every move rings a singing-bowl-like   
  tone.                                                                         
  Key insight to highlight in the UI and README: the Hanoi move sequence (stone 
  1,2,1,3,1,2,1,4,...)                                                          
  is a fractal "ruler sequence" — the recursion literally composes a            
  self-similar rhythm.                                                          
                                                                                
  Judging weights (prioritize in this order!):                                  
  Relaxation/UX 35% > Musical quality 25% > Creative use of recursion 15% >     
  Visual design 10% > Interaction/usability 10% > Technical execution 5%.       
  => Calmness and sound quality matter more than technical complexity.          
                                                                                
  Stack: Vite + vanilla JavaScript (ES modules), Tone.js, HTML Canvas 2D. No    
  3D, no frameworks.                                                            
  Everything runs in the browser. Synthesize all sounds with Tone.js (no        
  external audio files)                                                         
  so there are no licensing issues and export works reliably.                   
                                                                                
  Suggested structure:                                                          
  src/recursion/hanoi.js   – pure recursive generator, yields {stone, from, to, 
  depth, index}                                                                 
  src/audio/engine.js      – instruments, scales, effects, ambience, scheduling 
  src/audio/export.js      – WAV export                                         
  src/visual/scene.js      – canvas scene, parallax layers, stone animation,    
  ripples                                                                       
  src/visual/stack.js      – live recursion call-stack visualization            
  src/ui/controls.js       – parameter panel, keyboard shortcuts, share link    
  src/main.js, index.html, style.css

  ### 3.1 Configurable recursive algorithm                                      
  - A real recursive Tower of Hanoi function (not iterative), exposed as a      
  generator of moves                                                            
    carrying recursion depth info.                                              
  - Parameters: stone count (3–8), start peg, target peg.                       
                                                                                
  ### 3.2 Interactive visualization ("2.5D" canvas)                             
  - Parallax layers: dusk sky gradient with slowly appearing stars, distant     
  hills, a gently                                                               
    flowing river, foreground grass. Subtle, slow motion everywhere — nothing   
  jerky.                                                                        
  - Stones: smooth rounded river-stone shapes with soft shading and shadows,    
  each stone a slightly                                                         
    different earthy tone. Moves are animated in a slow, soft arc with easing.  
  - When a stone lands: a water ripple expands and a soft glow pulses (synced   
  with its sound).                                                              
  - Recursion panel: a minimal, elegant live view of the call stack (e.g.       
  "hanoi(4, A→C)") with                                                         
    the current depth highlighted, plus a small display of the fractal ruler    
  sequence building up.                                                         
  - Progress indicator: move X / (2^n − 1).                                     
                                                                                
  ### 3.3 Procedural music from the recursion                                   
  Mapping (make it sound genuinely beautiful and calm):                         
  - Stone size -> pitch: largest stone = lowest note, smallest = highest,       
  mapped onto the chosen scale.                                                 
  - Destination peg -> instrument: peg A = singing bowl (FM/metal-like soft     
  bell with long decay),                                                        
    peg B = kalimba/marimba-like pluck, peg C = warm soft pad. This creates     
  natural harmony.                                                              
  - Recursion depth -> reverb wet / delay feedback (deeper calls = more         
  spacious echo),                                                               
    so the recursion is audible.                                                
  - Stereo pan follows the peg position (left / center / right).                
  - A quiet drone on the root note underneath everything.                       
  - Scales/moods: Pentatonic Major (Calm), Hirajoshi (Zen), Raga Bhupali        
  (Bangla Dusk),                                                                
    Dorian (Dreamy). Root note selectable.                                      
  - Seed: a seeded random generator adds tiny humanization (velocity,           
  micro-timing). Same seed +                                                    
    same settings must reproduce the exact same composition.                    
  - Ambience mixer synthesized in Tone.js: rain (filtered noise with random     
  droplets),                                                                    
    river (low filtered noise with slow modulation), wind (band-passed noise    
  with slow sweep).                                                             
  - Audio rules: soft attacks, generous reverb, master limiter, master level    
  around -10 dB,                                                                
    no harsh high frequencies, smooth fade-in/out. Audio starts only after a    
  user gesture.                                                                 
                                                                                
  ### 3.4 UI with controls and playback                                         
  - Elegant glassmorphism side panel (collapsible) with: stone count,           
  scale/mood, root note,                                                        
    tempo ("breath pace": slow/medium/flow), peg->instrument presets, echo

    seed (with 🎲 randomize), ambience sliders, master volume.                  
  - Play / Pause / Restart / Step-forward buttons. Changing parameters          
  regenerates the piece smoothly.                                               
  - Presets: "Morning Mist", "Monsoon Evening", "Temple Night", "Deep Sleep".   
  - Share link: encode settings + seed in the URL hash so anyone can replay the

       55 +                                                              
       56 +> *The recursion doesn't just play notes — it composes. Every

  Part 1: recursion as musical form

  - The recursive generator now reports three kinds of event: a call starting, a
    stone moving, and a call ending.
  - Each call starting moves the harmony away from home (the question); each
    call ending moves it back (the answer).
  - The whole piece opens on the home chord with a soft bowl strike and closes
    on it with a final bowl and a long tail. The single-stone base case keeps
    its parent's chord.
  - Chords change about every 3–4 moves, the middle of the piece lands back on
    home, and phrases nest as 1, 3, 7, 15… moves.
  - A new pad plays the chords, with each voice gliding to the nearest note of
    the next chord. I tested every mood, root note and stone count: no clashing
    notes in 14,064 chord changes.
  - On strong beats (moves of stone 2 or larger) the melody settles on a note of
    the current chord.
  - Each phrase swells gently toward its middle and softens at its end. No extra
    notes are added, so the pulse is unchanged.
  - The recursion panel shows "asks" / "answers" on the move line, and the About
    modal has your quote and an explanation.

  Part 2: Sierpinski constellation

  - All 3ⁿ arrangements are drawn as dim stars in the upper sky (6,561 for 8
    stones). I checked that every arrangement gets its own position and that the
    shortest solution runs exactly along the bottom edge, from corner A to
    corner C.
  - The current arrangement glows amber, and a thin line follows the flying
    stone. When a solve finishes, the whole path glows, then fades over about 12
    s.
  - It has a panel toggle (triangle icon), is saved in the share link, and has a
    section in About the math. It dims while the breathing guide is on, because
    the guide's circle overlaps it.

  Part 3: sound polish

  - Low-cut at 80 Hz on everything except the drone.
  - Bells lose 6 dB above 7 kHz.
  - One shared reverb with a 7 s decay and 40 ms pre-delay.
  - Every change is ramped, the music fades in over 2 s, and the ambience keeps
    going between phrases and while paused.
  - At most 7 notes ring at once; older ones fade out.
  - Exports fade in over 2 s, fade out naturally, and are normalized to about
    −16 LUFS with peaks below −1 dBFS.

● Pause, Mute all, and auto-pause on a hidden tab all work. The build passes and
  it's committed as feat: mute-all control + auto pause on hidden tab. I
  checked every case below in a headless browser. A real hidden tab can't be
  produced there, so I simulated it, and I didn't listen for clicks; the fades
  are designed to avoid them (see below).

  1. Pause (Space): unchanged. The stones and music stop and the ambience keeps
     playing. While paused, a small "Ambience still playing" label sits just
     above the play button. It's hidden while sound is muted or the tab is
     hidden.
  2. Mute all: a new button with the volume-x icon, placed after step-forward,
     with keyboard shortcut M.
     - Muting pauses the stones, fades everything (music, drone, rain, river,
       wind) to silence over 1.5 s, then suspends audio processing to save CPU.
     - Unmuting resumes audio, fades back in over 1.5 s at your previous levels,
       and restarts the stones if they were playing.
     - Accessibility: it shows a pressed state, and its label switches between
       "Mute all sound (M)" and "Unmute (M)".
     - Play or Space while muted unmutes and starts playing.
     - Muting pauses the stones on purpose. Once audio is suspended its clock
       stops, so the stones would otherwise freeze mid-flight.
  3. Hidden tab: when you switch away, the same fade-out and pause happens. When
     you come back, it fades back in and resumes only if it was playing before.
     - A new settings toggle, "Pause when tab is hidden", is on by default and
       saved with your settings. It's left out of share links because it's a
       personal preference.
     - If you muted with M yourself, coming back to the tab keeps it muted.
  4. Shortcut lists: M is in the panel's keyboard list and in the README table.

  No clicks or pops: each fade starts from the exact current level, so reversing
  it halfway never jumps. The test caught fades midway at 0.43 and 0.53 of full
  volume. Audio is only suspended once the level is fully at zero, and muting
  then quickly unmuting never suspends at all.
