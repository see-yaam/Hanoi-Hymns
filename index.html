    Zen Stones

    Let the recursion carry the stones, and the river carry your thoughts.

    best with headphones · sound starts when you begin

    About the math

    The Tower of Hanoi asks: how do you move a tower of stones from one rock to another, one stone at a time, never placing a bigger stone on a smaller one?

    The answer is beautifully recursive. To move n stones from A to C:

    1. move the top n − 1 stones out of the way, A → B (the same puzzle, just smaller)
    2. move the biggest stone, A → C
    3. move the n − 1 stones back on top, B → C

    Each step calls itself on a smaller tower until only one stone is left. The moves double with every stone: 2n − 1 moves in total — 7 for three stones, 255 for eight.

    The fractal rhythm

    Write down which stone moves each time and you get the ruler sequence:

    1 2 1 3 1 2 1 4 1 2 1 3 1 2 1 5 …

    It looks like the marks on a ruler. Each block is two copies of the previous block joined by a new, larger number — the same pattern at every scale, a fractal. In Zen Stones that sequence is the melody and the rhythm: small high stones chime often, big low stones speak rarely, and after a big stone the river takes a longer breath.

    How you hear the recursion

    • Stone size → pitch: the largest stone is the lowest note.
    • Destination rock → instrument & position: left, centre, right.
    • Recursion depth → space: deeper calls echo further away.
